These are starting points, not rules. What really decides the size is how many names must fit at a size people can read from where they’ll stand.

How big should the names be?

A common signage rule of thumb is about 1 inch of letter height for every 10 feet of viewing distance for comfortable reading. Guests usually read a seating sign from 3–6 feet away, so names around ½ inch tall (roughly 36-point type) are comfortable, and table headings can be larger. If you need to shrink names much below that to fit, move up a size or split the list across two signs.

Rough capacity by size

With names around ½ inch tall and a heading at the top, an 18 × 24 sign comfortably fits about 3 columns of roughly 25–30 names each, and a 24 × 36 sign about 4 columns of 40 or so. Long names, decorative script fonts and big headers reduce that. Build your chart above and check the PDF export to see your real list.

Alphabetical or by table?

  • Alphabetical (name … table number): fastest for guests, because everyone looks for their own name. Best above about 80–100 guests.
  • By table (Table 1: names): looks more decorative and lets groups see who they’re sitting with. Best for smaller events.

Printing tips

  • Print from a PDF, not a phone screenshot, so text stays sharp at poster size.
  • Foam board is light and cheap; acrylic or a mirror looks upscale but needs a print shop or vinyl lettering.
  • Ask the print shop whether they need bleed (often ⅛ inch) and leave a margin so names aren’t trimmed.
  • Proofread names against your RSVP list and print a small test page at 100% to check readability.
  • Plan an easel: a 24 × 36 board needs a sturdy floor easel, and an 11 × 17 can sit on a tabletop stand.

Seating chart sign questions

What is the most common wedding seating chart sign size?

18 × 24 inches and 24 × 36 inches are the two most common poster sizes because they fit standard frames and easels and hold a typical guest list.

How far in advance should I print the sign?
