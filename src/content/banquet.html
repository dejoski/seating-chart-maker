How to make a banquet seating chart

A banquet, gala, corporate dinner or fundraiser is usually seated by table, not by exact chair, unless the dinner is plated and the caterer needs a meal at every place. Start with the people who must sit in a specific spot — the dais, a sponsor table, a host’s guests — and fill the rest of the room around them. The tool saves in your browser as you go.

  1. Import the guest list. Paste it from a spreadsheet or registration export. Use the Group column for company, ticket type or table host, and the Meal column if the kitchen needs counts. “Alex & Sam Lee” stays linked; “Jordan Park +1” adds a guest of Jordan Park.
  2. Choose the table shape. Under Tables → Quick layouts, pick rounds of 8 or 10, or long banquet tables. A 60-inch round usually seats 8 comfortably (10 is tight); a 72-inch round usually seats 10. A 6-foot banquet table usually seats 6–8 and an 8-foot table 8–10, including the ends. Confirm the count with the venue, because chair width and centrepieces change what fits.
  3. Set the head table and VIP or sponsor tables first. Add a head table for speakers, honorees or the planning committee, then name a few tables for lead sponsors, board members or a guest of honour. Seat those people and click the lock so auto-arrange will not move them.
  4. Add the rules that matter. Keep a donor with their table host, keep a party together, and keep two people apart when they should not share a table. Guests with the same Group are placed near each other when there is room.
  5. Auto-arrange, then walk the room. Auto-arrange fills the remaining seats while respecting locks and rules. Drag anyone who should move — a late VIP, a wheelchair space closer to an aisle, or a company that should sit nearer the stage.
  6. Print the chart and the lists for place cards. The PDF has a floor plan, a by-table list you can use to write or print place cards, and an A–Z list guests can scan at the door. The caterer CSV lists each table with every guest’s meal.

Banquets, galas, corporate dinners and fundraisers

  • Galas and awards dinners usually need a head table or stage-facing reserved tables, plus a clear path for speeches and a presenting line.
  • Corporate dinners often seat by team, client or region. Put hosts with their guests, and leave a spare seat or two at each client table for last-minute additions.
  • Fundraisers typically reserve the best tables for lead sponsors and auction paddles, then seat remaining guests by ticket package or table captain. Name those tables so the floor plan matches the signage in the room.

Head table, VIP tables and sponsor tables

A head table faces the room and holds the people who will speak or be named. VIP and sponsor tables sit closest to the stage, with a clear view and enough room for a reserved sign. Seat and lock those tables before you fill the general-admission rounds, so a later auto-arrange cannot shuffle a sponsor into the back row. If you do not need a formal head table, skip it and reserve the first one or two rounds instead.

Place cards, escort lists and signs

Most banquets assign tables rather than exact seats. Place cards, if you use them, can be set out from the by-table list after the chart is final. An alphabetical find-your-seat list is easier for guests once the room is larger than about 80 people; a by-table list is what the planner, caterer and place-card station want. The PDF export includes both, and the sign size guide covers how large to print the door sign.

Banquet seating chart questions

Do I assign seats or just tables?

Assigning tables is enough for most banquets, galas and fundraisers, and is what this tool does. Assign exact seats only for a formal plated dinner, or when the caterer must know where each meal goes; the caterer CSV still gives meals by table either way.

How do I keep a sponsor or VIP table intact?

Seat those guests first and click the lock on each name. Locked people stay put when you auto-arrange the rest of the room.

Can I start from a ready-made layout?

Yes. The seating chart templates include a banquet layout of long tables and party rounds. You can also change seat counts, rename tables and drag them to match the venue after you load a layout.

Is the guest list private?

Yes. It is stored only in your browser and never uploaded. See privacy.
