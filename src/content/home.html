How to make a seating chart

  1. Add your people. Paste names one per line, or paste columns from a spreadsheet (Name, Group, Meal, Notes). Write couples as “Alex & Sam Lee” and plus-ones as “Jordan Park +1” and they stay linked.
  2. Set up tables. Pick a quick layout (rounds of 8 or 10, long tables, head table, or classroom rows, pairs, pods and U-shape), or add tables one at a time with 1–20 seats each. Drag a table by its name bar to move it around the room.
  3. Add rules. Keep two people together or apart. Lock anyone whose seat is already decided.
  4. Auto-arrange, then fine-tune. Auto-arrange fills tables while respecting every rule it can, keeps couples together, and puts people from the same group near each other. Drag names to adjust (on a phone, tap a name, then tap a table).
  5. Export. Download a PDF (floor plan, by-table list and an A–Z “find your seat” list), a PNG image, or a caterer CSV with meals by table.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. Building, auto-arranging and exporting a chart is free, with no account and no limit on guests.

Where is my guest list stored?

Only in your browser’s local storage on this device. Nothing is uploaded. Use “Backup (.json)” to move a chart to another device, and “Restore” to load it there.

What happens when the rules can’t all be met?

The tool tries many arrangements and keeps the one with the fewest broken rules, then lists each conflict in red so you can add a table, change seat counts or loosen a rule.

Can I use it for more than one event?

Wedding, classroom and event modes each keep their own chart, so switching modes doesn’t overwrite anything. For more charts in the same mode, save a backup file for each one.

Does it work on a phone?

Yes. Dragging works with a mouse or pen; on a touch screen, tap a name to select it and then tap the table you want.
