The rules you can set

  • Keep together: the two people always share a table. Chains work too: if A is with B and B is with C, all three sit together.
  • Keep apart: the two people never share a table. In classroom mode they also won’t sit at neighbouring desks or pods.
  • Linked parties: couples (“Alex & Sam Lee”) and plus-ones (“Jordan Park +1”) are kept together automatically, without a rule.
  • Locked seats: click the lock on anyone already placed and auto-arrange will never move them.
  • Groups (soft): people with the same Group value are placed near each other when there is room. This is a preference, not a rule.

The rules tab is open in the tool above. Pick two names, choose “keep apart from” or “keep together with”, and click Add rule.

How auto-arrange works

  1. Links everyone who must sit together (parties and keep-together rules) into one unit.
  2. Places locked guests first, then the units with the most keep-apart rules, then the biggest units, because those are hardest to fit.
  3. For each unit, picks a table with enough free seats and no keep-apart clash, preferring tables with the same group and filling tables evenly.
  4. Repeats with many different orders and keeps the arrangement with the fewest broken rules.

It runs entirely in your browser in a fraction of a second for a typical wedding or class.

When rules conflict

Some rule sets can’t all be met. The tool tells you which, in red, instead of silently ignoring one:

  • Contradictions: two people linked as a couple or keep-together but also marked keep-apart.
  • Group too big: a linked group with more people than the biggest table. It gets split across tables, with a warning.
  • Not enough seats: more people than seats. Add a table or raise seat counts.
  • Too many keep-apart rules for too few tables: for example five people who must all be apart, but only four tables.

Manual moves are checked too: if you drag someone next to a person they should avoid, both names and tables turn red.

Questions about rules

Is there a limit on the number of rules?

No fixed limit. More keep-apart rules mean fewer valid arrangements, so the warnings will tell you if the set becomes impossible.

Can I keep someone at a specific table?

Yes: drag them to that table and click the lock. Locks always win over auto-arrange.

Does auto-arrange change seats I placed by hand?

It rebuilds the whole chart except locked guests. Lock anyone you placed by hand that you want to keep, or use Undo to go back.

Why did two people from the same group end up apart?

Groups are a soft preference. A hard rule, a lock or a full table can outweigh them. Add a keep-together rule if they must share a table.
