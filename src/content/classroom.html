Make a classroom seating chart in two minutes

  1. Paste your roster. One student per line, straight from your gradebook or a spreadsheet column. Only first names are fine.
  2. Choose a layout. Under Tables → Quick layouts pick Rows (single desks), Pairs, Pods of 4, or a U-shape. “Front / board” is at the top of the floor plan. Drag desks to match your room.
  3. Add keep-apart rules. In classroom mode a keep-apart rule means not at the same desk and not at a neighbouring desk or pod, so talkative pairs end up properly separated.
  4. Lock the seats that matter. Seat students who need to be near the board, the door or you, and click the lock so shuffles never move them.
  5. Shuffle. “Random shuffle” gives a new random arrangement every time while still keeping every rule and lock. Use it when you rotate seats each unit or term.
  6. Print. The PDF gives you a floor plan for your desk or a substitute, plus an alphabetical list.

Which classroom layout works for what

Tips from how teachers actually use seating charts

  • Start the year with a random chart: it’s fair, it helps you learn names, and nobody can argue with it.
  • After a couple of weeks, add the keep-apart rules you’ve learned and shuffle again.
  • Use the Group column for reading levels or language support, so auto-arrange can place similar students near each other in pods.
  • Keep one backup file per class period so each class has its own chart.

Classroom seating chart questions

Is the random seating chart generator truly random?

Each shuffle uses a new random order, then applies your locks and keep-apart rules. If you have no rules, the result is a plain random arrangement.

Are student names uploaded anywhere?

No. Names stay in your browser’s local storage on your device; nothing is sent to a server. That makes it easier to use under school privacy policies, but check your own district’s rules.

Can I do a chart for each class period?

Yes. Build one, click “Backup (.json)”, then start a new chart for the next period. “Restore” loads any period back.

What about students who must sit near the front?

Drag them to a front desk and click the lock icon. Locked students stay put when you shuffle.
